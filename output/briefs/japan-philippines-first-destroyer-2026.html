Japan and the Philippines Narrow Their Destroyer Talks to a First Ship

An Oct. 28 joint statement turns the Abukuma-class talks toward a first ship and puts Japanese training support before any transfer. Neither language version records a transfer decision.

By Benjamin Yang and Jophy Lin

The first ship

Japan and the Philippines have focused their latest destroyer-transfer talks on a first ship. After Japanese Defense Minister Koizumi Shinjiro and Philippine Defense Secretary Gilberto Teodoro Jr. met in the Philippines on Oct. 28, their joint press statement said the two defense authorities would work toward transferring the first Abukuma-class destroyer at the earliest possible date after its expected decommissioning "in the coming years."

The statement attaches two commitments to that ship. Japan will help prepare the Philippine Navy before the transfer, and the two sides will cooperate closely on maintenance and sustainment. Both the English and the Japanese text describe an understanding between the defense authorities, with the necessary coordination inside each government still to come. Neither version names a hull or a date.

From "the destroyers" to "the first"

The formal process dates to May. On May 5 the two ministers decided to discuss transferring defense equipment, including Abukuma-class destroyers, taking a Philippine request into account, and established a working group for the purpose. On May 31 in Singapore they reached a broad consensus to proceed with discussions toward transferring the destroyers "promptly after decommissioning." The working group's subjects then included training for the Philippine Navy, maintenance and sustainment, and the "proper management of the equipment after transfer."

Set beside those texts, October changes less than its framing suggests. The Japanese timing phrase moved from すみやかに ("promptly") to できる限り早い時期 ("the earliest possible date"), which still leaves the transfer open-ended. Maintenance remains a joint task and the working group remains the venue. The singular is what is new: one first ship, with decommissioning expected within a few years rather than at an unstated point. Post-transfer management, a May subject, is not mentioned in the October statement. The only item with a fiscal-year target in either record is a single TC-90 aircraft, which the May 31 statement targeted for transfer within Japan's fiscal 2027 and the October statement by the end of that fiscal year.

The two language versions also differ in firmness. The English text says the ministers "concurred in aiming for the transfer." The Japanese text says they agreed to continue consultations (協議を継続) toward it. Neither records a decision to transfer a ship.

Training before the transfer

Training moved furthest. In May it was one working-group subject among several. In October, the Japanese text says Japan will begin supporting Philippine Navy education and training ahead of the transfer. The English text reads more strongly, with Japan "providing the Philippine Navy with the necessary education and training prior to the transfer." The Japanese wording specifies support for training conducted by the Philippine Navy; the English does not make that division of responsibility explicit.

The statement links this to wider aims. It expects deeper cooperation in vessel operations, logistics support, human resource development and information sharing, and it describes defense cooperation, particularly in maritime security, reaching "a new level" (the Japanese text says 新たなステージ, "a new stage"). It sets no program or schedule for any of these.

The arrangements around the ships

An agreement to protect classified military information is still under negotiation. Those talks formally began in May. In the October statement, the ministers said there was an "urgent need to secure a foundation for information sharing, including the establishment of relevant institutional frameworks," and confirmed that their defense authorities would give the two governments' negotiations their "fullest cooperation," without setting a timetable.

One supporting arrangement is already in use. The Japan-Philippines Acquisition and Cross-Servicing Agreement (ACSA) lets the two militaries supply each other with goods and services. Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs treaty record shows it was signed in Manila on Jan. 15 and entered into force on Aug. 22. The October statement records its first use: during the Doushin-Bayanihan exercise, "the Japan Air Self-Defense Force received supplies from the Philippine Air Force pursuant to ACSA." The English statement dates the agreement's entry into force to July, as did a Philippine News Agency report on Oct. 29. The Japanese text says August, matching the treaty record. The treaty record dates the exchange of diplomatic notes, a separate step, to July 23.

Japan and the Philippines also co-chair the ASEAN Defence Ministers' Meeting-Plus Experts' Working Group on Maritime Security.

Five ships in Manila's account

The largest figures come from Manila. In July, Teodoro told reporters that the Philippines would receive five ships and called the arrangement a "done deal," Naval News reported, citing the Philippine News Agency. The same report described deliveries expected within two to three years, without directly attributing that forecast to Teodoro. None of the ministers' joint statements from May or October specifies a total ship count or delivery schedule for the destroyers.

Japan commissioned six Abukuma-class destroyer escorts between 1989 and 1993, each with a standard displacement of about 2,000 tons, according to Naval News. Japan also changed its transfer rules this year. On April 21 the government abolished the five permitted categories for equipment transfers (rescue, transport, warning, surveillance and minesweeping), "making it possible, subject to screening, to transfer any type of defense equipment," as the Japan Institute of International Affairs described it.

Two developments would show the talks moving from intent to a transfer. One is a Japanese decommissioning date for the first ship, since the statement makes decommissioning the point after which a transfer can happen. The other is the start of Japanese training support for the Philippine Navy, which the statement places before any handover.
