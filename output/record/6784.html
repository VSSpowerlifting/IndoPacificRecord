BEIJING, Nov. 26 -- "If Japan is unwilling to clean up the chemical weapons left behind during its aggression against China, how can it thoroughly root out the legacy of militarism?" said Senior Colonel Chen Xi, spokesperson for China's Ministry of National Defense (MND), at a press conference on Thursday. According to reports, China released a documentary titled Return Our Clean Land on Japanese Abandoned Chemical Weapons (ACWs) on November 23, which was screened concurrently during the Conference of the States Parties to the Chemical Weapons Convention. Some analysts point out that because Japan has dragged its feet on the destruction process under various pretexts, large quantities of its ACWs remain undisposed of to this day. The spokesperson noted that this year marks the 80th anniversary of the opening of the Tokyo Trial and the 29th anniversary of the entry into force of the Chemical Weapons Convention. During World War II, the invading Japanese forces developed and used chemical weapons against the Chinese people, committing untold crimes against humanity. "The documentaryReturn Our Clean Landdraws on extensive historical records, witness testimonies, and reflections and confessions from Japanese personnel. It recreates the heinous crimes of Japanese aggressors, reveals the lasting harm caused by Japan's ACWs, and underlines that the complete and thorough destruction of Japanese ACWs is a historical, political and legal responsibility that Japan must not shirk,"he said. Chen further pointed out that the Japanese government has long been passive and perfunctory on the issue of its ACWs, delaying the destruction process under various pretexts. Its political commitment to "complete and thorough destruction at an early date" risks becoming an empty promise. Worse still, Japanese right-wing forces, in defiance of international justice and human conscience, blatantly claim that the ACWs have gradually become "not harmful" and that "the legal statute of limitations for liability have passed". "Historical crimes must not be evaded, and responsibilities and obligations must not be shunned. We urge the Japanese side to provide effective information on burial sites at the earliest date, allocate more manpower, and accelerate the disposal process, so that the hazardous Japanese ACWs can be fully destroyed at an early date, and the Chinese people can live on a clean land," the spokesperson stressed.
